The induction turns onboarding into a signed-off session where new contractors watch each step on the big screen, do it on their phone in the demo property, and tick it off. Thirteen short modules, about three hours with a break, in the contractor's own language.

## How the induction runs

Every module has the same shape: three to five slides on the big screen, then "On your phone" in the demo property, then a short read-and-confirm block in their own language, then a tick in the app. Painters learn by doing, so nobody reads a document.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Who runs it | Tom or the Project Coordinator, in person, one to three new contractors at a time |
| Where | Big screen for the slides, each contractor on their own phone, Wi-Fi |
| How long | About three hours with one break. Can split into two sessions: modules 1 to 9, then 10 to 13 |
| Language | The contractor picks their language on the first screen: English, Dari (Afghan), Farsi, Arabic (Lebanese), Spanish, Portuguese (Brazil) or Italian. The wording they read and tick to confirm understanding is in that language, including the standards. The slides, the app and the demo job stay in English |
| Paid | No. The invite says so, so nobody asks on the day |
| The demo property | 12 Demo Street, a copy on every contractor's account. Same property, same planted events, so the slides always match the phone. Nothing in it counts toward their light or their money. "Reset demo" puts it back to the start |
| The check | No written test. Before they leave, the facilitator watches each contractor do five things without help: accept a job, add a before photo, move a surface to prepped, raise a variation, submit an invoice |
| The kit (facilitator only) | Roll of painter's tape, the laminated site card, and a sample board if one exists. The board is never mentioned on the slides |

**Slide rules.** One idea per slide. Short sentences. A picture wherever a picture will do. Every module ends with an "On your phone" slide that names the exact taps, numbered, in the order the demo expects them. Say "Paint Group" or "your Project Coordinator", never a person's name.

## The 13 modules in full

Each module below is written out as it runs on the day. **Slides** is what is on the big screen and what the facilitator says. **On your phone** is the exact taps in 12 Demo Street and what the contractor sees happen. **Check** is one question for the room before the tick. **Read and confirm** is the block the contractor reads in the app, in their own language, and ticks: it is the only part that is translated. **Tick** is the one-line statement that ends the module. **Worker version** says what changes when the module is run for workers.

## Module 1 · Welcome and your account (10 min)

The contractor leaves this module with a working account, their language chosen, and a clear list of what we still need from them.

**Slides**

1. **Welcome to Paint Group.** We find the work, quote it and look after the customer. You paint it to our standard. Three promises on every job: the customer always knows what is happening · the home is left clean · no surprises on price.
2. **How today works.** Slides on the screen. You do each step on your phone, in a practice job called 12 Demo Street. Tick each module when you have done it. About three hours, one break.
3. **What we need from you.** Public liability insurance · WorkCover if you have more than one worker · ABN and GST status · bank details · how many painters you bring.
4. **Haven't got your certificates with you?** That is fine today. The app shows what is missing and reminds you. Job offers start once your insurance is in.
5. **Choose your language.** What you read and tick to confirm is in your language, including the standards. The app and the demo job stay in English, with short words and pictures.

**What to say**

- "Thanks for coming. Today is about how we work together, so your first job runs smoothly. We find the customers and handle the money. You do the painting. Three promises matter to us on every job: the customer always knows what is happening, the home is left clean, and there are no surprises on price. Everything today comes back to those three."
- "You will not read a document today. You watch, then you do it on your phone. Nothing you do in 12 Demo Street is real. It is a practice job, so make your mistakes here, not on a customer's job."
- "We need these before we can offer you a job. Insurance is the one we cannot work around."
- "Don't worry if you haven't got them with you. Upload them from home tonight. The app will remind you until they are in."
- "Pick the language you read best. Everything you have to confirm today appears in that language. The app itself is in English, because the customer and the office use it in English, but the words are short and there are pictures."

**On your phone**

1. Open the link in your invite text. Choose your language. Everything you read and tick from now on is in that language.
2. Fill in your details: name, mobile, ABN, GST yes or no, bank details, and the number of painters you bring.
3. Upload your public liability certificate if you have it. If not, tap "I will add this later". It shows as Missing on your home screen until you add it.
4. Look at your home screen. 12 Demo Street is waiting there. Do not open it yet.

**Check.** Ask the room: what happens if you do not have your insurance yet? (You finish today. Job offers start when it is in.)

## Module 2 · How a Paint Group job runs (10 min)

The contractor can name the nine steps of a job and knows which ones are theirs.

2. **Who does what.** Paint Group: quotes, books, orders materials, talks to the customer, pays you. You: accept, paint, update the app, check your own work, run the walk-through.
3. **The walk-through is booked when you accept.** It is on the last day of the job. If the finish date changes, log it in the app the day you know, so we can tell the customer.
4. **Your home screen.** Your jobs · your status light · your documents · the Standards tab. 12 Demo Street is your practice job.

**What to say**

- "The split is simple. We do the customer and the money. You do the painting and the app. If you are ever not sure whose job something is, it is ours. Ask."
- "The customer gets the walk-through date the moment you accept. If you are going to finish early or late, the customer needs to know before the day, not on it. You log it. We tell them."
- "Open the app now. Everything you need on a job is on this one screen."

## Module 3 · In a customer's home (15 min)

These are the house rules. Most complaints come from behaviour, not paint.

**Slides**

1. **You are a guest in someone's home.** How you behave is half of what the customer remembers.
2. **Before you start.** Shoes off until the floors are covered. Cover every floor fully. On hard floors, use the floor protection we provide and tape the edges to seal the area. Ask: where to park, where to leave materials, may I use the toilet, power and water.
3. **While you work.** No smoking, no drugs, no alcohol. No eating inside an occupied home. Music only if the customer is happy, and ask first. Keep pets and children away from wet paint and ladders. Don't move valuables: ask the customer to.
4. **Every day at the end.** Materials packed in the agreed place and covered neatly. Rubbish in one place, never scattered. Floors stay protected until the last coat is dry. Wet-paint signs where needed. Doors and windows you opened for ventilation locked. Keys and alarm as agreed.
5. **Never.** Wash brushes in the customer's sink or garden (agree a place). Take photos of the home except the work. Post anything online. Bring anyone who is not on your crew. Open a tin before checking the colour schedule.
6. **Arriving.** Arrive at the agreed time. If you cannot make it or will be late, tell Paint Group the day before. On the day, before 7:30 am. We tell the customer. Never leave the customer waiting with no message.

**What to say**

- "The customer will not remember your cutting-in. They will remember whether you were polite, clean and on time."
- "Ask these questions on day one, before you bring anything in. Asking is respect. Guessing is a complaint."
- "Eat outside or in your van. Not sure if music is OK? Then no music. If something valuable is in the way, the customer moves it, not you. If it breaks in your hands, it is your problem."
- "The customer walks through their home every evening. What they see at 6 pm is what they tell their friends. Ten minutes of packing up buys you a happy customer."
- "Photos of the work go in the app. Nothing else, nowhere else. The colour schedule is on the work order. Check it before every tin, every time."
- "You never need to ring the customer about lateness. You ring us, early, and we handle it. Silence is the one thing we cannot fix."

**On your phone**

1. Open the induction and find Module 3, In a customer's home.
2. Read the site rules one at a time, in your language. Tick each one.

**Check.** Ask the room: it is 7:10 am and your van will not start. What do you do? (Call Paint Group now, before 7:30.) Can you eat your lunch in the customer's kitchen? (No. Outside or in the van.)

## Module 4 · Materials and equipment (10 min)

No job stops because something ran out, and nobody argues about who brings what.

**Slides**

1. **We supply the paint and everything you use up.** Paint, filler, caulk, tape, drop sheets, floor protection, sandpaper, rollers and brushes.
2. **You bring the tools.** Ladders, spray equipment, pots, trays, poles, sanding equipment, power tools. Anything you do not use up.
3. **Where the materials are.** We deliver to site where we can. Where we cannot, we order to the nearest paint shop for you to collect. The work order shows which.
4. **Short of something?** Tell us in the app or call. We order more. Never leave a job waiting because something ran out.
5. **Colour matches.** We arrange them before the job where we can. Sometimes we need you to go to the paint shop. We set it up first, so the right materials are ordered for the agreed scope.
6. **Loan gear.** Where we have it, we lend spray equipment, big ladders and scaffold. Collect it and return it in the same condition.

**What to say**

- "If it gets used up on the job, we supply it."
- "If it goes home with you at the end of the job, you bring it."
- "Check the Materials section of the work order the day before you start. It tells you if the paint is on site or waiting at the shop, and which shop."
- "Running out is normal. Not telling us is not. Tap Request more the moment you see you will be short, not the morning you need it."
- "Never match a colour yourself without talking to us. The customer approved a colour. We need to be sure it is that one."
- "Loan gear comes back clean and working, the same as you got it. If something breaks, tell us the same day."

## Module 5 · Finish standards (20 min)

This module is the approved painter guide, Finish Standards Version 1, taught one section at a time. The six ticks here are the standards sign-off itself. The slides come from the existing standards deck.

**Slides**

1. **The three levels.** Level 2: clean, tidy repaint. Repairs what is broken. Level 3: our standard finish. Fixes what a customer can see from 1.5 m. Level 4: premium finish. Fixes what a customer can see up close. The level is on your work order, for every surface.
2. **Where the customer judges your work.** Level 2: 3 m. Level 3: 1.5 m. Level 4: 0.5 m, arm's length. The customer has the final say. Our quality checks use the same distances.
3. **How it is judged.** Face the surface. Normal light: daylight, or the room lights on. No torch. High areas from the floor.
4. **Rules for every job.** No drips or runs. No paint where it should not be. No paint on hardware: remove it or mask it. Protect first. Full cover. Clean finish. Clean site every day, and clean site at the end.
5. **Your time.** Standard preparation is in your hours: covering, scraping, filling, gap filling, sanding, priming filler and bare patches. Bogging, stain blocking and additional coats are extra time on the work order. Anything not allowed for is a variation.
6. **One surface at a time: walls at Level 3.** Fill every hole, dent, crack and mark you can see from 1.5 m. Sand filler flat with soft edges, and sand the whole wall. Remove old drips and lumps you can see from 1.5 m. Lines sharp and straight from 1.5 m. Full, even cover: no patchy areas, no roller lines, no missed spots. Then the same slide for doors, then window frames.

**What to say**

- "Most of our jobs are Level 3. The level tells you how much preparation to do and how fine the finish must be. It is on every surface of your work order. If it is not there, ask before you start."
- "Stand at that distance and look. If you can see it, fix it. The customer will stand there too."
- "Nobody judges your ceiling from a ladder with a torch. From the floor, in normal light. That is how we check, and that is how you check."
- "These do not change with the level. A Level 2 job with a drip on the tiles is a fail. If you spill or drip, clean it straight away. If it will not come off, tell us the same day."
- "You are never paid extra for standard prep. You are paid extra for bog, stain block and extra coats, and the work order shows the hours. If you find something that needs them and it is not on the work order: stop, photos, variation. Module 8 covers that."
- "Every surface has its own list in the app, at every level. The list is the rule. Open it on the job and work down it. We will do three surfaces here. You read the rest on your phone."

**On your phone**

1. Open the induction and find Module 5, Finish standards. Read and tick each of the six sections: The three levels · Rules for every job · Your time · Interior surfaces · Exterior surfaces · The defect rule. Each section is in your language. The tick is at the end of each one.
2. Open 12 Demo Street, tap Walls, tap What we expect. This is the Level 3 list for walls, the same one you just saw on the screen.
3. Stand 1.5 m from the nearest wall in the room and do the look test. What can you see from there? That is what you would fix on a Level 3 job.

**Check.** Ask the room: the work order says Level 3 and there is an old drip you can only see from 30 cm. Do you fix it? (No. It cannot be seen from 1.5 m.) The same drip on a Level 4 job? (Yes.)

## Module 6 · Quality checks and your status light (15 min)

The contractor knows what a clean job is, what each colour means, and that this is a reward system, not a fine.

**Slides**

1. **What a clean job is.** The app kept up to date, from the reminder prompts. Every quality check passed first time. No call back in the 7 days after sign-off.
2. **Who gets checked.** New painters: the first 3 jobs. Yellow: 1 job in 3. Orange: every job. Green: only when we choose.
3. **The five colours.** New (blue), Green, Yellow, Orange, Red. Four clean jobs in a row take you to Green. After that, your last ten jobs decide your colour. Jobs under 16 hours do not count.
4. **What each colour gets you.** Green: priority on new jobs, paid within 3 business days of sign-off, bonus eligible. Yellow: normal offers, normal payment. Orange: offers after Green and Yellow, a check on every job, a call from your Project Coordinator. Red: no more job offers. We meet with you to go through the level of finish expected on your current job.
5. **Lost it? Win it back.** Four clean jobs in a row take you back to Green, from any colour.
6. **This is a reward system.** It never takes money off your payment.

**What to say**

- "Three things, and all three are in your hands. A job counts as clean seven days after sign-off."
- "A quality check is your Project Coordinator looking at the job with the same look test you use. Pass first time and it counts for you. A fail you fix the same day is still a failed check, but it is not a call back."
- "You start on New. Four clean jobs in a row and you are Green. After that, your last ten jobs decide your colour. Small jobs, under 16 hours, are not scored either way."
- "Green is where everyone should be, and the money shows it: paid in three business days. Red means we stop offering you work until we have met and gone through the finish on your current job. Jobs already in progress finish."
- "No colour is permanent. Four clean jobs, from anywhere, and you are Green again."
- "Nothing here is a fine. Green gets you more. The other colours only change how closely we look and how we offer work."

## Module 7 · Talking to the customer (15 min)

Most disputes start with a painter answering a question that belonged to Paint Group.

**Slides**

1. **Who talks about what.** You: hello on day one, what is happening today, simple questions about today's work, keep the site clean, update the app. Paint Group: price, scope, the level of finish, variations, colour changes, dates and walk-through bookings, complaints, call backs, invoices, customers who are not judging the work fairly, customers who need more reassurance, requests for extras outside the scope.
2. **What the customer hears at each stage.** Booked: we confirm the dates. Day one: you say hello and explain the plan for the day. Work days: your app updates become their progress updates. Finish date change: you log it, we tell them. Last day: the walk-through. After: we send the invoice and the report.
3. **Running late or can't come.** Tell Paint Group the day before. On the day, before 7:30 am. We tell the customer. Never silence.
4. **Finish date changing?** Log it in the app the day you know. We update the customer and the walk-through booking.
5. **When a customer is unhappy.** 1 Stay calm and polite. Do not argue. 2 Listen. Let them finish. 3 Look at the area together, from the right distance for the level. 4 Fix it now if it is our standard and quick. 5 Anything else: no promises. Take photos and note it in the app. 6 Call Paint Group the same day. We take it from there.
6. **Small extras are encouraged.** Never agree a price, a big extra or a change of scope on site. Say: "Good question. I will ask Paint Group to call you today."

**What to say**

- "Your side of the list is short on purpose. Everything else you pass to us. Not because we do not trust you. Because one voice on price and scope means no surprises for anyone."
- "Your app updates go to the customer as progress. Every photo and every tick is a message to them. That is why keeping the app up to date matters so much."
- "Same rule as Module 3. It matters enough to say twice."
- "Early is a change too. A customer who planned to be home on Friday does not want the walk-through on Wednesday with no warning."
- "Six steps. The only hard one is not arguing. Look at it together from the right distance. Most of the time that settles it. If it does not, you are not the one who settles it. We are."
- "A small extra, five minutes, no materials: do it. It makes the customer happy. Anything bigger, that sentence. Learn it word for word."

## Module 8 · Variations (15 min)

A variation logged the moment it is seen gets paid. A variation raised at the end of the job starts an argument.

**Slides**

1. **The scope is the job.** The work order lists every surface and the hours for it. Anything not itemised in the scope can be charged as a variation.
2. **Fair and reasonable.** Small items missed from the scope are part of the job, and what is small depends on the job. 2-day job: a missed window frame is a variation. 3-week job: 5 metres of fascia, or 1 door in 50, is expected. Do it. A whole room, a ceiling not in the scope, or a second colour is always a variation. Not sure? Ask before you start it.
3. **Log it immediately.** The moment you see something missing or extra, log it. Not at the end of the job.
4. **The loop.** You log it with photos and hours → Paint Group prices it → the customer approves → it comes back to you to accept → it is added to your invoice. Never start the extra work before the customer has approved.
5. **What is never a variation.** Standard preparation. Paint applied too thin. Something you missed in your own work.

**What to say**

- "Read the scope before day one. Walk the house with it on day one. What is on it, you paint. What is not on it is a question: not a freebie, and not a fight."
- "We do not use a number, because no number is fair on every job. Ask yourself: on a job this size, would a reasonable painter expect this to be included? If you are not sure, that is what Ask a question is for."
- "A variation raised on day one gets priced and approved while you work. A variation raised on the last day cannot be approved before the walk-through, and the customer feels ambushed."
- "Five steps, and you are in two of them: log, and accept. Everything in between is us and the customer. Do not start until it comes back approved."
- "Standard prep is in your hours. A third coat because the second was thin is on you. Bog, stain block and a real extra coat for a colour change are extra time. If they are not on the work order, that is a variation."

**On your phone**

## Module 9 · Pre-defect, walk-through and call backs (20 min)

The customer should find nothing. The painter who finds their own defects never gets a call back.

**Slides**

1. **Why we pre-defect.** The customer should find nothing. Fewer defects at the walk-through. The customer sees we are one step ahead. You finish in one visit and do not come back.
2. **The tape check (jobs of 16 hours or more).** Finish painting and clean the site. Walk the job on your own and do the look test on every surface. Put tape next to every defect. Fix each one and take the tape off. No tape left means ready.
3. **What counts as a defect.** Runs, drips, sags, missed spots, thin patches, old colour showing through, roller lines. Preparation not done to the level: holes, cracks and gaps not filled, filler not sanded flat. Wavy lines at ceilings, trim and glass. Paint on glass, hardware, floors, switches. Dust and hairs in the finish. Windows and doors stuck. Hardware not put back.
4. **Before the walk-through.** Site clean, rubbish gone, floors uncovered, hardware back, windows and doors open and close, final photos in the app.
5. **How to walk the customer through.** Start at the front door. One room at a time. Let them look from the distance for the level. If they point at something small, fix it there and then. Anything bigger: note it in the app, no promises, Paint Group calls them. Then they sign on your phone.
6. **What a call back is.** A return visit on another day to fix workmanship. Fixed and signed the same day is a pass, not a call back. A customer call back counts for 7 days after sign-off. Only workmanship counts against you.

**What to say**

- "Every defect you find is one the customer does not. Every defect the customer finds costs you a return visit and costs us trust."
- "Walk it the way the customer will: room by room, from the right distance. The tape is for you. It stops you forgetting one. Under 16 hours you still check everything; you just do not need the tape."
- "This is the list your Project Coordinator uses on a quality check. Use the same one."
- "The customer should walk into a finished home, not a work site with good paint."
- "You lead the walk-through. Front door first, every room, let them look. A small mark, fix it in front of them. That is the best thing a customer can see. Something bigger: write it down, say we will call, and we do."
- "A call back is not a punishment. It is a return visit. Fix it on the day and it is not one. Something the customer finds in the first week, we come back, and it counts. Something that is not your work, like a leak or damage after you left, does not count."

**Do it in the room.** The facilitator puts three pieces of tape on the wall. Each contractor finds them, says what they would fix, and takes the tape off.

**On your phone**

1. On 12 Demo Street, complete the finish checklist (eight ticks) and add a final photo.

3. Sign the demo sign-off. The job moves to Sign-off, then to Invoice.

**Check.** Ask the room: the customer finds a drip at the walk-through, you fix it in ten minutes and they sign. Is that a call back? (No. Passed after a fix.) They ring three days later about a missed patch behind a door. (Yes. A call back, and it counts.)

### Read and confirm

1. I find and fix my own defects before the customer sees them.
2. On jobs of 16 hours or more I tape every defect, fix it, and take the tape off. No tape left means ready. On smaller jobs I still check every surface.
3. Before the walk-through: site clean, rubbish gone, floors uncovered, hardware back, windows and doors open and close, final photos in the app.
4. I lead the walk-through from the front door, one room at a time. Small things I fix there and then. Anything bigger: a note in the app, no promises, and Paint Group calls the customer.

## Module 13 · Safety basics (10 min)

Short, and it belongs in any induction. Nothing here replaces a white card.

**Slides**

1. **Ladders and heights.** Footed, on a level base, three points of contact. Big ladders and scaffold: ask us, we lend them.
2. **Old homes.** Houses built before 1970 may have lead paint. Sheet walls and eaves may be asbestos. If you think you have found either: stop, do not sand or scrape, call us.
3. **Dust and fumes.** Sand with extraction or wet. Ventilate. Wet-paint signs where people walk.
4. **Your crew.** You are responsible for everyone you bring. They work to the same rules, and your light covers all of them. Every worker needs a white card.
5. **Something unsafe at the home?** Exposed wiring, a dangerous roof, an aggressive dog: stop and call Paint Group before you continue.

**What to say**

- "Nobody falls off a ladder that is footed and level. Big ladders and scaffold are ours to lend, so there is never a reason to stretch."
- "Powdery old paint on an old house, or flat grey sheeting with a dimpled pattern in the eaves: stop and ring. We get it tested. Nobody sands it until we know."
- "Windows open, extraction on, and signs up so the customer's kids do not walk into a wet door."
- "Your worker's drip is your drip. Your worker's call back is your call back. Bring people you trust, and make sure they do the worker induction."
- "You are never expected to work through something unsafe. Stop, ring us, and we sort it out with the customer."
