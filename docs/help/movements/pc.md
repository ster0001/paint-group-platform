## What this is for
**The tray is everything that has to move** this week — paint per brand, materials per line, gear, rubbish — built from the jobs. Amber = not yet planned. Anything can also be added from scratch. **Drag a chip onto a person's day.** Supplier lane = delivered (you place the order). A painter's lane = they pick it up or move it. Hourly lanes (Felipe's and Tom's, set in Settings → Movements) are in hours. Tick **Ordered** and the painter is told. **Nearby** on a day means there are chips close to where that person already is; tap a **While you're there** suggestion to drop it; open any chip for its shop, time and note.

The project coordinator uses it on a laptop on Thursday or Friday to plan the week ahead, and on a phone to drop one more chip from the van. Open it from **Projects → Movements** (the address is /pc/movements). Until Wednesday it shows this week; from Thursday, next week.

## Before you start
- Chips come from the jobs on their own: a paint chip per brand from the job's Materials card (one line per product), a materials chip per line on the job's materials list, and one **Rubbish — how?** chip per job. Gear chips you add yourself from the register, because the register does not know what a job needs.
- Who gets a staff lane, and whether that lane is in hours or AM/PM, is **Settings → Movements → board people** (Session 6 ships the screen; until then the office sets it). Painters appear as lanes when they have a job booked in the week.
- Paint can be **planned** before the colours are confirmed, but not **ordered** — the chip says so in amber.
- A delivery needs a shop that delivers. The Supplier lane refuses a chip whose shop is counter-only and tells you to pick another shop in the chip or drop it on a painter.

## Steps

### What the tray shows
1. The ‹ › arrows move the board a week; the lanes and the tray follow without a reload.
2. **×** on any tray chip removes it in one tap: a materials line chip takes its line off the job page and the estimate too; any other chip is cancelled and the sync does not bring it back. A removed paint chip does not come back by itself: make a new one with **+ Add a chip**.
3. One group per job, with the painter and the dates with their month (*Saul · Mon 27 Oct–Wed 29 Oct*). A job with no dates has nothing to plan yet, so its chips (and its check-ins) stay out of the tray until the job is booked.
4. A pickup dropped on a morning gets 07:00 as its time (Settings → Movements → default times), an afternoon 13:00; open the chip to make it any time of day. The Supplier lane keeps plain AM / PM.
5. The month and the days stay at the top while you scroll down the lanes.

### Dropping a chip on a person
1. A drop on a painter's or a staff person's lane opens a **confirm box** first: who it is going to, the **day** and the **time** (both editable), and a tick for **Send a calendar invitation**. Nothing is placed until you press Confirm; Cancel leaves the chip in the tray. The Supplier lane still places straight away.
2. With the tick on, that person gets a **text** saying what to do, where and when (*Please collect the paint (Dulux · 2 lines) from Dulux Trade Oakleigh — Mon 27 Oct at 07:30–08:30*; *Please collect the rubbish from 1 Test St*; *Please drop off the gear at…*) and an email with an .ics invitation: the stop sits in their phone's calendar with the shop (a pickup) or the site as the place. Since 24 Oct 2026 the calendar entry's **title is the instruction itself** (*Please collect the paint (Taubmans - 2 cans) from Dulux Trade Oakleigh and deliver it to 6 Fleet St (PG-0101)*, not *Paint · Taubmans · 2 cans*) and its **description carries the same words as the text** — the day and time, the place, the order reference, the note and the link — in plain letters, so no stray symbols appear on the phone. Moving the stop and confirming again sends an updated invitation that replaces the first in their calendar. The toast says who it went to, or why it did not (no email on file, or the automation switched off in Settings → Automations → "Movements — calendar invitation for a stop").

### Planning the week
1. Open **Projects → Movements**. The headline says how many chips are still in the tray and how many movements are placed; **Orders to place** counts every placed delivery or pickup whose order you have not yet ticked. **‹ ›** moves a week at a time. The **How this tab works** strip opens on your first visit and folds away after; open it again from its title.
2. The **tray** on the left lists every chip, grouped by job (closed jobs with gear still on site are their own group; **Warehouse** chips last). Filter with **All · Paint · Materials · Gear · Rubbish** (the counts are live) or search by chip, job or suburb. An amber edge means not yet planned; a clay edge means the chip cannot be placed yet and the line under it says why (gear waiting on a return, a rubbish chip you have not answered).
3. The **board** is lanes × days: **Supplier delivers** first, then every painter booked this week, then the staff lanes. A painter's or the supplier's day splits **AM over PM**; an hourly lane shows **ten one-hour rows from 07:30**. What everyone is already doing is drawn in: green **On site** blocks from the schedule, violet calendar entries, cyan quality checks, job check-ins and walkthroughs, grey leave. A lane whose Google Calendar could not be read says so under the name instead of showing an empty day.
4. **Drag a chip** onto a half or an hour row. While you drag, the cells near where that person already is light cyan, the cell under your pointer lights green, and the ghost names the lane and the time; a red ghost says why the drop would be refused. Drop into an hour row and the movement takes that time. Drop a chip before its job ends (a return) or after it starts (a delivery) and the board places it but warns you.
5. The chip becomes a **card** on the day: the kind of movement (Paint delivery, Paint pickup, Gear drop-off, Gear collection, Skip in, Rubbish to warehouse …) in the lane's colour, the shop, the time, the job reference and a state line. A supplier order or a painter's pickup shows amber **Not ordered yet** with an **Ordered ✓** button; a staff-lane drop is booked by placing it. **Done** ticks it; **↩** sends it back to the tray (so does dragging it onto the tray).
6. Tick **Ordered ✓** once you have placed the order with the shop. That is what tells the painter: a pickup texts them the shop, its address, the from-time and the order reference; a delivery texts them what is coming and when. The texts are **drafts** until Tom switches them to automatic in Settings → Automations (Movements — your pickup is ready / a delivery is coming), so until then approve them from the queue. The client is told nothing unless you turned **Client notice** on in the chip. A paint order is refused until the colours are confirmed.

### Nearby and While you're there
1. A staff lane's day shows a cyan **Nearby N — see** pill when tray chips sit within the Settings radius (7 km by default) of somewhere that person already is that day. Tap it for the list, nearest first, and tap a chip to drop it on that day at the time of the appointment it is near.
2. Under a calendar entry or a quality check, one dashed **While you're there** suggestion names the single nearest tray chip. Tap it to drop it.

### The chip sheet
1. Tap any chip, in the tray or on the board. The sheet shows where it is placed and how it moves ("delivered to site", "Josef picks up", "Felipe drops off"). On a paint, materials or gear chip, **Open in PC Command →** at the top takes you to the same job's page, whose Materials & movements card links back to the chip on the board — the two screens are a tap apart for one job.
2. **Paint**: one chip per brand per job — every product of the brand is a line on the one order, and a product whose brand was left blank takes the brand its name starts with. The lines are the job's Materials card for that brand — tap a line to change its colour name or litres (that edits the card, the same litres the job page's Materials card and the estimate builder's working scope edit; the chip re-reads it and recounts the cans). Once ordered, the lines are frozen as they were at ordering.
3. **Materials**: the line text is the same row the estimate and the job page show. One line is one order — write the items on the line separated by commas (up to 500 characters). A line with several items shows each item as a bubble with its own ×: that × takes the item off this order into a new chip of its own (tapes out of stock — the rest of the order stays as it was). No pickup place is assumed for a materials order: the tray says *no pickup place yet — tap to set the places and how it moves*, and the Supplier lane refuses it until you have. In the sheet (since 23 Oct 2026) a paint or materials chip has **two places and four ways to move**. **Pickup place** is the list of shops (paint first, then hardware, then saved places, nearest first) with a search box and **+ Type an address** — type a name and an address and it is saved as a place for every list from then on. **Drop-off place** is the job site unless you change it: **Our warehouse**, **+ Another address**, or **Use as drop-off** on any place in the list. **How does it move?** — **Pick up**: someone collects it at the pickup place and brings it to the drop-off place (drop the chip on whoever); **Drop off**: it comes from our warehouse to the drop-off place, nothing to order; **Pick up and deliver**: collected at the pickup place and delivered to the drop-off place, both named on the run and in the painter's text; **Delivered by the shop**: the shop drives it — this chip goes onto the Supplier lane on the job's first day (tick Ordered ✓ once the shop has the order). Only a delivery goes on the Supplier lane; the other three are ours to carry.
4. **Gear**: the items, each with where it is now. Every chip — gear, rubbish, hire, the lot — has the same **Pickup place** and **Drop-off place** rows as paint and materials (since 23 Oct 2026): the job site, our warehouse, a saved place from the list, or **+ Another address**; the tray line reads *warehouse → site* or *site → warehouse* so a return or a collection says where it goes. A gear chip typed as words on the job page (the **Gear needed** box under Equipment movements booked — one line per chip, commas between items) has no register items; booking it still makes its return chip.
5. **Rubbish — how?**: **Painter disposed** (nothing to move, recorded on the painter's last day), **Painter drops at warehouse** (a drop-off on the painter's lane on their last day), **Left on site** (the chip stays in the tray as a collection — drop it on whoever collects, or make it a **Skip** from the chip with a hire yard and the in and out days).
6. **Shop** (or **Hire yard**): for paint and materials every paint shop comes first, nearest first, then the hardware stores — never Bunnings by default — and the search box narrows by brand, branch or suburb. **Time** chips are the hourly rows on an hourly lane, otherwise early-morning times and AM/PM; the exact-time box takes any time of day, and **How long** (15 minutes to 4 hours, an hour unless changed) sets the stop's end time on the run plan. **Order reference** is printed on the supplier's order and is what the invoice matches itself to. **Client notice** is off unless you turn it on for that delivery. Add a **note** for the shop or the painter.
7. **Ordered ✓** (when the order step is owed), **Copy order text** (paint with confirmed colours — paste into the email or the trade app), **Back to tray**, **Remove** (a chip you added by hand), **Save**.

### Site check-ins to book
1. A site check-in that has no day yet (one made from the job page, or a job's standing check-in nobody has placed) sits under the tray as **Site check-ins to book**, one per job.
2. Drag it onto a staff lane: the hour row (or the morning / afternoon on an AM/PM lane) is the time. The check-in is booked for that day and time and leaves the tray; the job page and the staff calendar show it as before. It refuses a painter or supplier lane, a day that has gone, and a time after the final walkthrough.

### + Add a chip — from scratch
1. Tap **+ Add a chip** under the tray (or on the Sources view). Pick **Paint · Materials · Gear · Rubbish**, then the job (or **Warehouse**).
2. **Materials** on a job writes a line on the job's materials list — so it is on the job page and the estimate too — and the chip appears from that line. **Gear** shows the register with each item's current location; anything not at the warehouse cannot be ticked. **Rubbish** is refused for a job that already has its chip. **Paint** from scratch is a chip with your words; the office confirms the Materials card.

### Sources — one list, three doors
1. Switch to **Sources** and pick a job. Three cards show the same job: the **estimate's** crew notes, its internal Materials & sundries list and the Materials card's paint rows; the **job page's pre-start** with the two gate ticks (Materials ordered, Equipment movements booked), the paint chips per brand and the same materials list; and the **tray**, with this job's chips, live.
2. Add a line in any card and it appears in all three and becomes a chip in the tray. Remove it anywhere and it is gone everywhere — unless its chip is already on someone's day, in which case bring the chip back to the tray first.
3. The strip under the cards names the doors: the estimate, the job page, the painter's request (Accept as a line) and the tray.
4. Every list shows the orders as **bubbles**: tap the words to edit the line in place, × removes it, and the × on an item inside a multi-item bubble splits that item into its own order. The estimate builder's Materials & sundries field works the same way — type an order, press Enter, it is a bubble.
5. Since Session 7 the doors are wired: the estimate's internal **Materials & sundries** lines become lines on the job when it is issued; **Accept as a line** on a painter's materials request (job page) makes a line and a chip; and the two pre-start ticks on the job page are real gates over the chips and tick themselves (since 24 Oct 2026: Materials ordered ticks the moment every paint and materials chip is off the tray — placed on a day or the Supplier lane — or a Not needed / Painter supplies answer is given; Equipment movements booked needs every gear chip booked). The painter's completion-prep yeses (Equipment for collection, Rubbish for collection) confirm the job's return and rubbish chips instead of raising a card. An off-hire ticked Done with an expected cost writes the hire cost to the job; a supplier invoice carrying the order reference links itself to its order.

### Day — run plans
1. Switch to **Day** (or tap **Orders to place** in the header). Pick a day — each chip says how many moves are on it — and a person; the people chips are everyone with a stop that day, in their lane colour.
2. The timeline runs from the **warehouse** out: fixed appointments (a quality check, a calendar entry) are dashed with ◌; movements are numbered, with the kind of movement, what it is, where it goes and the kilometres from the previous stop. The header totals the stops and the km.
3. **▲▼** move a stop earlier or later. The times move with the stops — the same set of times, in the new order — so the Week board shows the same thing. **Ordered ✓** on a stop that still owes its order works here too; the big **✓** ticks a booked stop done.
4. **Send to <name>'s phone** texts the person their run for the day with a link: staff open their own list (Navigate, Ring, Done, Problem on each stop); a painter opens the **Your pickups and drop-offs today** card in the portal. Every press sends again, because the run can change.
5. The **Orders to place** card lists every placed delivery or pickup in the week whose order is not yet in, soonest first, each with its own **Ordered ✓** (or **Booked ✓** for a hire or skip). A paint row whose colours are still TBC is disabled and says so.
6. At 6 pm, a supplier delivery due today that nobody ticked Done texts the job's painter "did it arrive?" (draft-first, like the others).

### Map — plan a run
1. **Plan a run** sits above the order card: pick whose run (any person on the board) — the day is the one selected. The box starts empty.
2. Add stops three ways: **+ Add to run** on a job icon's popover, **+ Add** on a chip in **Still to plan** (every tray chip, by job, under the map), or drag an icon or a chip into the box. A job that has a chip in the tray adds that chip with its own reason; every row has a **what for** list — **Collect materials · Quality check · Site check-in · Rubbish collection · Drop off materials · Drop off paint** — and the reason is yours: keep the chip's own (marked *this chip*) and the stop is that chip; pick another and publishing makes a **new chip** for that reason while the original stays in the tray. **Open chip** on a chip's row opens its sheet, so a paint or materials pickup gets its shop before you publish (the row warns *no shop yet* until it has one).
3. **Rubbish on an office run is a collection**: publishing a rubbish chip on Felipe's or Tom's run marks it *collected on the run* — the bags ride in the car until the warehouse, with no separate warehouse trip and no tip run.
4. The first stop starts at 07:30 unless the calendar already holds that time, then it takes the next free quarter hour; each later stop starts when the one before ends. Every stop runs an hour unless you change **How long**; the start time can be typed. Drag the handle (or ▲▼) to reorder — the times re-slot in the new order. × removes a stop, **Clear** empties the box.
5. The map draws the run as a solid line in the person's colour from the warehouse through the stops and back. Nothing is on anyone's day yet — the plan lives in your browser until you publish.
6. **Publish this run** puts every stop on that person's day at its time in one go: a chip is placed (it is that chip), a purpose stop becomes an Other chip with that title, and a **Site check-in** books the job's check-in that had no day yet (or makes a stop when the job has none). The Day view and the week board show the same rows; the run plan text to the painter is sent from Day or here as before.

### Map — one icon per job, and the day in order
1. Switch to **Map** (the tab is there once the Google Maps browser key is set — Settings → Movements says whether it is). Pick a day and a person as on Day; the people chips are everyone with a stop that day.
2. **Only the active jobs are on the map**: jobs on site or in the finish (in progress, quality check, completion prep, walk-through) and closed jobs with gear still on them, which drop off a week after the job's invoice is paid. Offers and bookings confirmed are not drawn. **Every job is one icon, chosen by the most important thing open on it** (since 25 Oct 2026): **red with a warning sign** when something is wrong (a movement overdue or with a problem, a payment hold, a walkthrough the customer flagged, a call back not yet booked); **violet with a phone** when the client needs a call (a check-in or follow-up due, a call-back visit coming up, a delivery they have not confirmed); **cyan with a clipboard** when the office needs to be on site (a quality check due or to book, a site check-in due); a teal house when nothing is outstanding; **amber, carrying the icon of what is needed** (paint, materials, gear, rubbish) when that job still has a chip in the tray or a placed order not yet ticked Ordered; a grey house for a closed job with gear still on it. **Hover or tap an icon** for the street, suburb, job reference and one line — "paint to plan · gear to plan", "paint delivery · not ordered", "on track", "closed · gear still here"; a tap keeps it open, a second tap closes it. On an amber icon the popover has an **Open …** button that opens the chip it is waiting on.
3. The **warehouse** is the dark square. Shops are faint dots that show only with the **Shops** toggle on, except a shop that is a stop today, which is drawn solid and named.
4. The selected person's **order numbers** sit on the icon of each stop of their day, in their lane colour — on the shop for a pickup, on the job for a delivery or a return. A fixed appointment (a quality check, a calendar entry) shows **◌** in violet. **Route line** draws straight lines from the first stop to the last in the lane colour (since 25 Oct 2026 the day starts at its first stop, not the warehouse — the warehouse is on the map but not on the line); it is off by default.
5. The **order card** beside the map lists the day's stops as rows: number, what, from → to, time and km from the previous stop (the first stop is the start of the day and shows no km; the total in the heading is the legs between stops, with no warehouse leg out or back). **Drag the ⋮⋮ handle** (mouse or finger) to put a stop above or below another; fixed appointments have no handle and stay at their time. The times move with the stops — the same set of times, in the new order — so the map, this card, the Day view and the Week board all show the same thing. **Run plan** opens the Day view on that person and day; **Send to <name>'s phone** sends the run as Day does.
6. A job is placed at its real address once the map has looked it up (it does this on its own, through the office's Places key, the first time a job is on the map and once a week after a miss); until then it sits at its suburb's centre.

### Gear — the register
1. Switch to **Gear**. Filter **All · At warehouse · Out on site · With a painter · Flagged**; the legend shows the nine type icons (step ladder, extension ladder 7 m and 10 m, platform ladder, spray gun, Planex sander, Festool vacuum, mobile scaffold, fall protection).
2. Each card is one item: its type icon, name and tag, where it is now and what carries it next, and a pill — **Warehouse** (green), **On site** / **On site · return booked** (cyan), **With painter**, **Repair**, or **On site · job closed** (clay — flagged). Location is never typed: it is the last ticked movement that carried the item.
3. Tap a card for its trail (every movement that carried it, done or planned). A flagged item has **Find it in the tray**, which opens its return chip on the Week board so you can drop it on whoever is passing; **Edit item** goes to Settings → Equipment.

### Settings — Suppliers, Equipment, Movements
1. **Settings → Movements → Suppliers**: every paint shop, hardware store and hire yard by brand. Filter by kind; tick **delivers**, **favourite** (first in the chip sheet) and **active** on the row; **Edit** for the address, suburb (its centroid places the shop), opening time and notes; **Add a branch** for a new one (its brand's vendor is created with it). Type the **trade account reference** in the box and press **Set**: it is saved to its own staff-only table and shown masked (••••1234) from then on — it never appears on a painter's screen or in the repo.
2. **Settings → Movements → Equipment**: the register. **Add item** with a name, type and serial; the tag (E-###) is given on save unless you type one. Change an item's status (active · repair · retired · lost) on the row; an item on a planned movement cannot be retired until that movement is finished.
3. **Settings → Movements → Movements**: the warehouse (label, address, suburb, postcode, optional lat/lng), the **Nearby radius** (7 km by default), the **planning horizon**, the **delivery confirm time**, the default AM / PM times, the hire-cost switch, the strip, and **board people** — who gets a staff lane, **AM / PM or 1-hour slots**, their colour, and whether quality checks and check-ins sit on their lane. The folder also says whether the Google Maps browser key is set: the Map view stays off until it is.

### On a phone
The board scrolls sideways with the lane names pinned on the left. The tray is a drawer at the bottom: tap **Tray** to open it, drag a chip up onto the board, and it closes behind you.

## What the colours and labels mean
- **Amber** — a chip not yet planned; a card whose order has not been placed (**Not ordered yet**); colours TBC on a paint chip.
- **Cyan** — ordered or booked; a Nearby pill; a quality check or job check-in block.
- **Green** — the cell you are about to drop on; an On site block from the schedule.
- **Violet** — a Google Calendar entry (read-only).
- **Clay** — a chip that cannot be placed yet, with the reason; a movement marked as a problem.
- **Grey** — leave; the supplier lane's colour.
- Each lane has its own colour dot; the same colour labels its movements everywhere.

## If something goes wrong
- **A line at the top says something could not be read** (the movements, the schedule, Google Calendar, the branches). The board draws what it could; nothing is silently empty. Reload; if it stays, send the line to Tom.
- **"That branch does not deliver to site"** — open the chip and pick a delivering shop, or drop the chip on a painter who can pick it up.
- **"The supplier lane is for orders. Drop our own gear on a person."** — our ladders and sprayers move with a person, never a supplier.
- **"That gear cannot go out until its return is done"** — the item is still at its last job; the return chip is in the tray, drop it on whoever collects it first.
- **"Say how the rubbish goes first"** — open the rubbish chip and answer the question.
- **"Confirm the colours before ordering the paint"** — finish the colour consult on the job page; the chip can stay placed meanwhile.
- **No staff lanes** — nobody is in Settings → Movements → board people yet.
- **No Map tab, or the map says it is off** — the Google Maps browser key is not set for this deployment (Settings → Movements → Movements shows which). The order card still works from Day.
- **The map says it could not load** — the key's website restriction does not include this address, or the Maps JavaScript API is not enabled on it; the line on the map says which to check.
- **A job sits at its suburb's centre, not its house** — the address could not be found by the lookup; it is tried again a week later. Check the job's address on the job page.
- **The tab has to go for a while** — Settings → Movements → Movements → untick **Show the Movements tab**. The tab and its screens are hidden; nothing is deleted; tick it again to bring them back.
