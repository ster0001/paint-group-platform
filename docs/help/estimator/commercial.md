## What this is for
Commercial painting is not one thing. A few offices can be priced from a desk; a strata building or a hospital needs someone to see it first. The online estimate sorts that out on the first commercial screen, so you are never asked questions that do not apply and never shown a number we would not stand behind.

## Before you start
Know which kind of space it is and roughly its size. For a warehouse: the floor area (a bracket, or length × width) and the height to the underside of the roof. For anything with a meeting date (a strata committee), have the date.

## Steps
1. The first screen: the address. The place screen: choose **Commercial**.
2. **What kind of space?** Eight cards. Each carries a tag — **Online · or we visit** or **We visit**. Tap one; the picture on the right changes to that kind of building (an office block, a warehouse, a shop, a clinic, a school, an apartment block, a row of shop fronts, a hall). **Which part?** underneath asks Inside, Outside or Both. Outside or Both on any commercial job means a visit — heights and access cannot be read from a form — and a box says so straight away.
3. **An "online" space, inside** (office, warehouse, retail and hospitality, aged care and clinics, schools):
   - **Areas** — how many of each kind of area, with a box for the open spaces (size, ceiling, a photo) and "Also being painted?" chips. The picture is a plan with one block per kind of area, and it follows your counts.
   - **The warehouse** has its own screen instead: floor area, height (above about four metres we allow for a scissor lift), what is being painted (walls, the underside of the roof, steel, roller and personnel doors, offices inside…), the wall materials, and three access questions. The picture is the inside of a warehouse; each surface you tick takes its fresh coat, and the height label follows your answer.
   - **The job** — surfaces, what is changing colour, the condition in your building's own words, when we can work, and whether it stays occupied.
4. The last question asks where to send it. Then **See my guide range** — labelled with your kind of space. The range is deliberately a little wider for commercial work; the note under it says why. **No price is fixed online for a commercial job**: tighten it area by area if you like, then your estimator confirms it, usually without a visit.
5. **A "we visit" space, or any outside work** (strata, shop fronts, something else, a hospital, every commercial outside): **Questions** — what needs painting, a few questions for that kind of building (a date only when the answer needs one), photos if you have them (optional, but they help a lot), and anything else we should know. Then **Book** — how the visit goes, in three numbered steps, the times we have, and where to send the confirmation. **Book it**.
6. **Booked** — a large tick and "You're booked in" with the time (or "Your brief is with us" if you did not pick one and we will call), then **What happens next**. Nothing is fixed until you say so, and nothing is owed.

## What the colours and labels mean
- **Online · or we visit** on a card — a range now; a person confirms it.
- **We visit** on a card — a brief and a booking; no number until the visit.
- **Amber** notes on the range — things widening it (no photos yet, an exposed ceiling, after-hours work); each says what would tighten it.

## If something goes wrong
- The kind of space you need is not there — pick the closest, or tap **Book someone in** and tell us what it is.
- No booking time suits — **Book it** without a time and we ring within one working day, or call the number on the screen.
- Anything urgent — the number is on every screen.
