# Post-wizard buildout — the order, the reasons, the playbook

## The logic in one line
**Follow the job, then follow the money, then follow the customer.** Everything on your list hangs off one spine — a job moving from accepted → done → paid — so we build the spine first and attach organs to it, instead of building seven disconnected modules and wiring them later.

Also worth saying before the order: you're closer than the list implies. Work orders v1 is built; the contractor portal core (offers, responses, calendar, contractor invoicing) shipped in the scheduling phases; and the approved-pending `acceptance-to-paid-workflow.md` already designs most of #1, #2 and the mid-job half of #5. **First action: approve that doc and answer its five flags — it unlocks phases 1 and 2 below.**

## The order

**Phase 1 — Work orders completion loop (#2, and half of #7).** WO v2/v3 per the existing mockup: per-surface status ticks, before/after photo prompts, variations (approved vs awaiting), completion walkthrough → customer sign-off. *Why first:* the ticks are the raw material every other module consumes — they become the customer portal's timeline, the trigger for the final invoice, the content of completion reports, and the contractor's daily surface. It's also the smallest step (v1 exists) with the biggest visible professionalism gain. Completion reports fall out of this almost free: sign-off + photo sets + variations = the report, generated not written.

**Phase 2 — Invoicing & payments (#1).** Deposit invoice auto-raised on acceptance → progress claims (commercial) → final invoice fired by Phase 1's sign-off → the G5 auto-chase ladder (+3 email, +7 email+SMS, +10 escalate, pause flags). Payments via Stripe payment links on every invoice (tap-to-pay from the SMS/portal). *Why second:* this closes the cash cycle on jobs you're already winning — the highest-ROI module on the list — and it needs Phase 1's sign-off event to know when "final" is. Contractor-side invoicing already exists from the portal build; this is the customer side. **Decision needed up front: payment provider (Stripe recommended) and surcharge policy.**

**Phase 3 — Customer portal (#3).** Now it has real content instead of placeholders: the live timeline (Phase 1 ticks), payable invoices (Phase 2), the estimate + colour records, defect flagging and sign-off (already mocked in the homepage demos), documents. *Why third and not earlier:* a portal before Phases 1–2 is an empty room. It also gates the website launch — the homepage demos promise this portal, and the rule stands that the site may not demo vapourware.

**Phase 3.5 — The experience website (parallel track).** The website wasn't in the seven because it sits on the demand axis, not the delivery axis — and its conversion engine (the wizard) already ships first, as master-plan Step 10: **launch-minimal means the wizard live on a clean landing page with the existing WordPress site linking to it ("Get an instant estimate")** immediately after the proving window, so the funnel is on weeks before the experience site exists. The full v5 experience homepage then builds as a **parallel track beside Phase 4** (it touches no money tables) but launches only **after Phase 3**, because its strongest sections — live timeline, records browser, defect pin, sign-off — are demos of the portal, and the standing rule is the site never demos vapourware. Launching after the portal turns every demo into a preview of something real, and the CRM lead inbox exists to catch what the site generates. ⚑ decisions due at this phase: SEO migration plan (36 suburb pages + review history must redirect/stage, not big-bang), and the sampler public-ballpark call from the website plan.

**Phase 4 — CRM, deliberately lightweight (#4).** A pipeline board over the estimate states you already have (draft → sent → viewed → accepted/declined), a lead inbox (wizard drop-outs, callback requests, out-of-area enquiries), an activity log per contact, and source/suburb attribution. *Why fourth:* follow-ups (Phase 5) act on CRM states, so states must exist first. **The trap to avoid:** do not let this become a Salesforce clone — your CRM is the estimate list wearing a pipeline view, not a second product. If a CRM feature doesn't change what someone does this week, it's cut.

**Phase 5 — Auto follow-ups & remarketing (#5).** Pre-sales: sequences on Phase 4 states (estimate sent + 3 days no view → nudge; viewed not accepted → the objection-handling touch; drop-out → resume link). Mid-job: mostly already designed — the G3 updates console (drafts from ticks, PC reviews and sends). Post-sales: review request at sign-off, the colour-record "we still have your colours" touch, and the sleeper asset — **the 2-year warranty anniversary as a remarketing calendar** (12-month check-in, 22-month "warranty ending, book your inspection"). *Rules carried over:* draft-only first month before anything auto-sends, and the SMS/email provider decisions (Twilio/ClickSend, Resend) finally get made here if they haven't been.

**Phase 6 — Materials & expenses (#6).** Receipt/expense capture against jobs, contractor invoices (already flowing from the portal) landing as job costs, pass-throughs (traffic management, scaffold, skips) with the courier/movement costs from the G-phases, and the payoff screen: **job P&L — estimated vs actual**, per job and per rate item. *Why sixth rather than earlier:* it needs completed, paid jobs flowing through Phases 1–2 to have anything to measure — but don't skip it, because actual-vs-estimate is the calibration data that makes your pricing engine sharper every month. This is the module that turns the platform from operations into intelligence.

**Phase 7 — Contractor portal completion (#7).** Mostly polish by now: Phase 1 already rebuilt their daily surface (the WO), their invoicing exists, so what remains is the standards library + academy content (mostly *your* writing, not code), quality scores wired to QA outcomes, and the Money views. Runs partly as the parallel track — the fill-in work whenever a main phase is blocked on you.

**Dependency picture:** 1 → 2 → 3 are strictly sequential. Wizard-on-a-landing-page goes live before all of them (Step 10). The experience website builds in parallel with 4 but launches after 3. 4 can start in parallel with 3 (different tables). 5 needs 4. 6 needs 2. 7 threads throughout. Realistic shape: each phase is 1–3 weeks of disciplined PRs; the spine (1–3) is the quarter's backbone, with the experience site landing right behind it.

## The Claude Code playbook (what the rebuild taught us, applied forward)

1. **No module starts without a plan doc + reference mockup committed first** — and the kickoff ritual stays: "commit these, confirm the file list back to me before writing code." I'll produce the mockup + brief per phase as we reach it, same as the wizard.
2. **Walking skeleton first, always.** Thinnest end-to-end slice before depth: one deposit invoice with a working Stripe link before claim schedules; one tick reaching the portal before the full timeline. A skeleton proves the wiring while it's cheap to fix.
3. **e2e-first, in the real role.** Every PR starts with a failing journey spec driven as the actual user — anonymous customer, contractor JWT, PC login. The payload-by-role bug lives forever as the reason.
4. **Money rules are absolute:** integer cents, every amount derived server-side (invoice totals from the accepted snapshot + approved variations via `lib/pricing` — no module ever computes money independently), one transaction per money event, golden tests green on every branch.
5. **One design system, reused.** Amber = awaiting, cyan = confirmed/active, the confirm-loop and tile patterns — point every brief at the existing mockups so the portal, CRM and invoices feel like one product, because they are.
6. **One branch per feature, mockup checklist per PR, health check (Part C) between phases, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` updated per module.** Never two parallel branches on the same tables.
7. **Surface decisions before the build, not during.** Each phase brief opens with its ⚑ list (payment provider, surcharge, SMS provider, review platform, chase-ladder timing) so Claude Code never invents a business decision to keep moving — inventing-to-unblock is how the wizard went wrong.
